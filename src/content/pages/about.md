---

*Sensus fidei* is an old phrase for something ordinary: the instinct by which people
who actually practice a faith come to recognize what's true in it, together, over time —
not by vote and not by expertise, but by living inside it long enough that the shape of
things becomes familiar. I didn't pick the name to sound impressive. I picked it because
it describes what I'm actually doing here, which is smaller than it sounds: paying
attention, over years, to how the faith and the news and the dishes in the sink all sit in
the same life.

I read the news most mornings, the way most people do, and most mornings something in it
needs sitting with rather than reacting to. This site is where I do the sitting with. I
write about doctrine and prayer, about money and work and what we leave behind, about
marriage and children and the unglamorous mechanics of a home, about the culture we're all
actually living in, and about labor and land and what a job is for. I write from inside
the Catholic tradition, and I lean hard on Aristotle and Aquinas when I need a place to
stand, because I've found that the oldest tools are often still the sharpest ones for the
newest problems.

I am not a priest, a financial advisor, or an attorney, and nothing here should be read as
professional counsel of any kind — least of all the essays on money and property, which
are about frameworks and values, not your particular return, your particular contract, or
your particular family. Where a decision needs a license behind it, go find someone
licensed. What I'm offering instead is a way of thinking about the questions underneath
those decisions.

New essays go up here first. Some of them make their way to Substack afterward, and I
share links on Pinterest and occasionally LinkedIn, but this site is the actual home of
the thing — not a landing page pointing somewhere else. If you're new, the five sections in
the header are the whole map: [Faith](/faith/), [Stewardship](/stewardship/), [Family](/family/), [Culture](/culture/),
and [Vocation](/vocation/). Start wherever your week has left you.
